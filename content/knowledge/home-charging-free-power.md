# Free and cheap home charging

*Last checked: 15 November 2026.*

An EV battery is the biggest flexible load most homes have, and it doesn't care what time of day it gets filled. That makes it a perfect match for the newer electricity plans that give you **free power in the middle of the day** or a **cheap rate overnight**.

This page covers the three main options, what's available in your state or territory, and how to point your Zeekr at the cheap hours. It's the money side of home charging; for the electrical side, see [Home charging explained](home-charging.md).

!!! tip "The short version"
    - **Home during the day?** A free midday plan (three or four free hours) can fill most of your weekly driving for nothing.
    - **Car away all day?** An EV plan with a cheap window around midnight to 6am is usually the better fit.
    - Either way you'll need a **smart meter**, and you'll set a **charging schedule** so the car only draws power in the cheap window.
    - Compare plans on [Energy Made Easy](https://www.energymadeeasy.gov.au) or, in Victoria, [Victorian Energy Compare](https://compare.energy.vic.gov.au) before you switch.

## Option 1: the government's free midday power

### Solar Sharer Offer (NSW, south-east Queensland, SA)

The federal **Solar Sharer Offer** gives eligible households 3 hours of free electricity in the middle of every day [^1]. It became available on 1 July 2026 [^2].

- **Where:** New South Wales, South Australia and south-east Queensland (the areas covered by the Default Market Offer) [^1].
- **When:** 11am to 2pm in NSW and south-east Queensland, and 12pm to 3pm in South Australia [^1]. The window stays at the same clock time through daylight saving [^3].
- **How much:** up to 24 kWh free each day. Anything over that in the window is charged at the plan's cheapest rate for other times of day [^1][^3].
- **Who:** households with a smart meter, renters and owners alike, not supplied through an embedded network. You don't need solar panels [^1].
- **Which retailers:** every retailer with more than 1,000 customers in those areas must offer it. It's opt-in, so you have to ask for it [^1].

The free hours don't make the whole bill free. You still pay the daily supply charge and for power used outside the window [^1]. Rates outside the window are set to the same annual price as the regulated time-of-use offer [^4], so it pays off when you actually shift your usage. The regulator lists charging an EV in the middle of the day as one way to do that [^4].

### Midday Power Saver (Victoria)

Victoria runs its own version, the **Victorian Midday Power Saver**. It started on 1 October 2026 and gives three hours of free electricity every day between 11am and 2pm [^5], weekends included [^6].

- You need a smart meter, and most retailers must offer it [^6].
- It's optional, and power used outside the free window costs slightly more, so check [Victorian Energy Compare](https://compare.energy.vic.gov.au) before you sign up [^6].
- Ask your retailer about any daily cap on the free usage before you plan big charging sessions around it.

### The rest of Australia

The Solar Sharer Offer doesn't cover the ACT, Tasmania, the Northern Territory or Western Australia. The federal government is working with those jurisdictions on a possible expansion [^1]. In the meantime, some local retailers have their own versions (see the [state-by-state table](#state-by-state)).

## Option 2: retailer "free hours" plans

Some retailers go further with their own plans, usually **four free hours**. Two examples, not a full market survey and not an endorsement:

- **OVO Energy, The Free 4 Plan:** $0.00/kWh from 11am to 3pm every day, in Victoria, NSW, Queensland and South Australia. Smart meter required, controlled load excluded, no daily cap but a fair use policy applies [^7].
- **GloBird Energy, FOUR4FREE:** free from 11am to 3pm every day on standard circuits (not controlled load), for homes with an eligible smart meter in Victoria, Queensland, NSW and South Australia [^8].

Outside the free window you pay the plan's normal rates [^7], and those can be higher than a regular plan's. The free hours are only half the story, so compare the whole plan.

## Option 3: EV plans with a cheap overnight window

If the car is at work with you all day, the midday plans don't help much. **EV time-of-use plans** give you a very low rate overnight instead, usually around **midnight to 6am**. Examples:

- **AGL Night Saver EV:** a low EV rate from 12am to 6am. You must drive an EV and have a digital (smart) meter, and the rate doesn't apply to controlled load [^9].
- **OVO Energy, The EV Plan:** a low rate from 12am to 6am, in Victoria, NSW, Queensland and South Australia, for homes with a smart meter and an electric vehicle [^10].
- **ActewAGL evEnergy Saver (ACT):** a low rate from 12am to 6am seven days a week, plus two free hours on Saturdays and Sundays between 12 noon and 2pm. You need an EV and a smart meter [^11].
- **Synergy Electric Vehicle Add On (WA):** an Overnight period from 11pm to 6am, and an even cheaper Super Off Peak period from 9am to 3pm. The EV must be registered with the WA Department of Transport at your address [^12].

A few things to watch:

- Some plans ask for proof that you own an EV, such as rego at your address [^12].
- Getting a smart meter can change your network tariff, which can change the rates you pay outside the EV window [^9]. Ask your retailer before the meter goes in.
- Six hours on a 7 kW wallbox is about 42 kWh, which covers a lot of driving (calculated: 7 kW x 6 h, before charging losses).

## How to set it up with your Zeekr

The trick is to plug in whenever you're home and let a schedule decide when the power flows.

1. **Get the plan right first.** Confirm you have a smart meter, sign up, and note the exact free or cheap window on your plan.
2. **Set a schedule in the ZEEKR app.** The app can check charging status, start and stop charging, and set scheduled charging [^18]. For the in-car option, look in the charging settings on the centre screen or check your owner's manual.
3. **Or use your wallbox's schedule.** If your wallbox has its own app, it may have a schedule or off-peak setting. Set the schedule in one place only (the car or the wallbox), or they can fight each other and you'll find the car didn't charge.
4. **Give yourself a margin.** Start a few minutes after the window opens and finish a few minutes before it closes, in case the clocks disagree.
5. **Check the first bill.** Make sure the charging shows up in the free or cheap period.

### What a free three hours is worth

Here's a quick example on a **7 kW single-phase wallbox**:

- 7 kW x 3 h = **21 kWh** added (calculated, before charging losses).
- The 7X Long Range RWD (MY26) has a claimed consumption of 17.7 kWh per 100 km [^19].
- 21 kWh at 17.7 kWh per 100 km is about **120 km** of driving (calculated). Your mileage may vary, literally: highway speeds, cold mornings and a heavy right foot all bring it down.

Do that most days and you've covered a typical commute for nothing beyond the supply charge you pay anyway.

If you have a **three-phase** charger, watch the Solar Sharer Offer's 24 kWh daily cap [^1]. An 11 kW charger running for the full three hours adds about 33 kWh (calculated), so you'd pay for the part over the cap.

## Before you switch

!!! warning "Tariffs change, so check your bill"
    Plans, windows and rates change, often once a year and sometimes sooner. The free hours only save money if you use them, and the rates outside the window can be higher. Before you switch, put your own usage into [Energy Made Easy](https://www.energymadeeasy.gov.au) (it can use your last 12 months of data) or [Victorian Energy Compare](https://compare.energy.vic.gov.au), and check your first bill after switching [^3][^6].

- **Daytime windows suit people who are home in the day**, or whose car sits at home on a schedule (work from home, school runs, a second car).
- **Weekly chargers may not gain much.** If you only charge your EV once a week or so, that alone may not justify a Solar Sharer plan [^20].
- **Solar owners: check the feed-in tariff.** A free-hours plan can pay you less for the solar you export than your current plan does [^20]. If your panels already cover your midday use, the sums work out differently.
- **Shared house?** The 24 kWh cap on the Solar Sharer Offer covers the whole home, not just the car [^1]. Running the dryer and the dishwasher in the same window eats into it.
