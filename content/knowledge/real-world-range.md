# Range in the real world

*Last checked: 15 November 2026.*

The range on the brochure is usually a lab figure. It is good for comparing cars and not a promise for your commute. Here is how far apart they usually sit, and why. It's also important to realise that range can vary greatly from day to day, even with the same driver and roads, and with the seasons (heat and cold), so _your mileage may vary_!

## WLTP vs real driving

**WLTP** is a standard laboratory test cycle that lets you compare cars on the same basis. Zeekr's published Australian figures are WLTP: for example 405 km (X RWD), 627 km (7X Long Range MY27) and 604 km (009 FWD) [^1][^2][^3].

Australia's official range figure under ADR 81/02 uses the older NEDC cycle, and the Green Vehicle Guide still lists it, while many makers advertise WLTP [^4]. Its replacement, ADR 81/03, accepts WLTP testing and applies to new models from 1 July 2026 and to all models from 1 July 2028 [^5].

Independent Australian testing by the AAA's Real World Testing Program, run over one fixed mixed circuit, found BEV range 5% to 14% below the makers' claims across five cars [^nrma]:

| Car | Claimed range | Shortfall |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Smart #3 Premium | 455 km | 23 km (about 5%) |
| Tesla Model Y Long Range | 533 km | 43 km (about 8%) |
| Kia EV6 Air | 528 km | 44 km (about 8%) |
| BYD Atto 3 Extended Range | 420 km (WLTP) | 51 km (12%) |
| Tesla Model 3 RWD | 513 km | 72 km (about 14%) |

Percentages marked "about" are calculated from the published kilometre shortfalls [^6]. No Zeekr was in that test, so don't assume your car lands in this band.

## What eats your range

The figures below come from different tests on different cars. Read them as the direction and rough size of each effect, not a rule for your Zeekr.

### Speed

Drag rises sharply with speed. In one test of a Skoda Enyaq iV, going from 96 km/h to 112 km/h cost about 48 km of estimated range, from an estimate of 372 km [^7]. That is roughly 13% (calculated). An owner review of a Hyundai Ioniq 5 also found efficiency dropping above 100 km/h, with a bit over 400 km on the highway [^8].

### Cold

With the cabin heater off, EV driving range was about 12% lower at 20 °F (about minus 7 °C) than at 75 °F (about 24 °C). With the heater on, the AAA's 2019 testing put the drop at 41%, and about two-thirds of the extra energy went to heating the cabin [^9]. Preheating while plugged in and using seat heaters instead of the cabin heater are the suggested workarounds [^9]. These are older US figures, so use them as a guide only.

### Wind and rain

Drag rises with the air speed over the car, so a headwind costs range, and wet roads add rolling resistance. Allow extra margin on windy or wet days.

### Roof loads

A ski rack cost about 19% of range in the Enyaq test [^7]. The same test gives no separate figures for a roof box or bike rack. The US Department of Energy's advice is to remove an external cargo container, such as a roof box, when you are not using it [^10].

### Towing

- A towed camper trailer cut a Hyundai Ioniq 5's range by about a third, to about 290 km, with speed held to 90 to 100 km/h [^8].
- Towing a caravan gave less than half the original range in the Enyaq test [^7].
- The 7X is rated to tow 2,000 kg braked and 750 kg unbraked [^11]. A towing rating says nothing about how far you will get, so plan charging stops with the heavy-load numbers above in mind.

## What helps your range

Some of the energy you spend getting moving can come back, and a few habits make a real difference.

### Regenerative braking

When you lift off the accelerator or press the brake, the motor runs as a generator. It turns the car's **kinetic energy** (its movement) back into electricity and stores it in the battery, instead of wasting it as heat in the brake pads [^12][^13]. This is **regenerative braking**, or **regen**.

It has limits:

- **It can't recover everything.** Hard braking calls in the friction brakes, which recover nothing, so look ahead and slow gently [^14]. Regen is an aid, not a replacement for the brake pedal: brake whenever the situation needs it [^13][^15].
- **A full battery takes less.** Regen is limited when the battery is full, to protect it [^16]. How much your Zeekr recovers depends on the battery's state, the regen level and your speed [^13][^15].
- **A cold battery takes less too.** Battery performance drops in cold weather, and that reduces regen [^9].

Where it helps most:

- **Stop-start city driving**, where you slow down often [^12][^17].
- **Long descents**, where gravity keeps the car rolling and regen holds your speed [^18].
- **Less at steady highway speed.** There's little braking to recover from, so regen offers little advantage there [^12][^17].

### How Zeekrs do it

Every Zeekr on sale in Australia has regen, and you can adjust it.

- **7X (MY25/MY26):** brake energy recovery is standard on every grade [^19]. In the **Driving** menu on the centre screen you set regen to **Standard** or **Strong** and switch **one-pedal mode** on or off [^13][^20].
    - **Strong** slows the car quickly when you lift off. **Standard** is gentler and smoother for passengers [^13].
    - In one-pedal mode, lifting off slows the car to a stop and Auto Hold keeps it there [^13]. The mode is smooth rather than abrupt [^20].
    - One-pedal mode switches off while adaptive cruise or lane centring is on, with hill descent control on, or outside Drive [^13].
- **X (MY25):** its custom drive modes include regen [^21]. The **Driving** menu on the centre screen has two regen levels and a **Stop mode**, whose **Maintain** setting slows the car to a stop and holds it with Auto Hold [^15].
    - For one-pedal driving around town, pick the high regen level and the standstill one-pedal mode. You may still need a light touch of the brake to stop in time [^22].
    - The car remembers one-pedal mode between drives [^22].
- **009 (MY25/MY26):** it has selectable regen levels and one-pedal driving [^23]. Regen is linear and not harsh [^24]. Look for the settings in the car's driving settings, or ask your dealer to show you at handover.

When regen slows the car hard, the brake lights come on to warn the driver behind [^13][^15].

### Other things that help

- **Precondition while plugged in.** Heat or cool the cabin while the car is still on the charger, so the trip starts with the battery doing less work [^9][^14].
- **Keep your tyres at the right pressure.** Correct pressures cut rolling resistance [^13][^15].
- **Drive smoothly at a steady speed.** Avoid hard acceleration and braking, and leave a gap so you brake less [^13][^15]. Higher speeds cost range, as above.
- **Use the gentler drive setting.** On the 7X (MY25/MY26), the **Comfort** acceleration mode gives smooth power and longer range [^13]. On the X, set acceleration to the subdued mode [^15]. Economy modes save energy by limiting performance such as acceleration [^14].
- **Take the roof racks off when you're not using them.** Racks and roof boxes add drag, as above [^7][^10].
- **Clear out weight you don't need** [^13][^15].

## Planning rules that work

- Plan on less than the WLTP number. A 5% to 14% haircut is what independent Australian testing found [^6], and highway speed, winter and towing take more.
- Keep a buffer at the next charger. See the [road trips guide](../guides/charging/road-trips.md).
- Preheat the car while it is plugged in [^9].
- Let regen do the slowing: ease off early for lights and corners. See [regenerative braking](#regenerative-braking).
