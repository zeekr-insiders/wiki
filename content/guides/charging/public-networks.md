# Public Charging in Australia

*Last checked: 8 November 2026.*

Australia has several charging networks, a couple of plug types at the AC end, and chargers that are sometimes out of action. Here's what to sort out before you need it.

!!! info "2027 7X (MY27)"
    The updated 2027 7X (MY27) delivers from December 2026. Figures on this page are for the MY25/MY26 car unless marked. See [Which 7X do I have?](../../models/7x/which-7x.md).

## Before your first public charge

### Carry a Type 2 cable

- **DC fast chargers** have the cable attached. Plug in and go.
- **AC chargers** at shopping centres, hotels and car parks often have only a socket, so you need your own Type 2 to Type 2 cable. Keep it in the car.

### Set up the apps at home

Don't wait until you're standing at a charger to create an account and add a card. Install:

- **PlugShare** for a map of chargers across networks, with recent check-ins from other drivers. If the last few check-ins say a unit is broken, believe them.
- **The app for each network you're likely to use.** Which networks are near you varies by state; see your [state resource page](../../resources/index.md).
- **Tesla**, if you plan to use Superchargers that are open to other EVs. The Tesla app shows which sites accept non-Tesla cars.

## How fast your Zeekr can charge

| Model | Peak DC charging |
| :--- | :--- |
| 7X RWD | up to 450 kW [1] |
| 7X Long Range and Performance AWD | up to 420 kW [1] |
| X RWD (MY26) | up to 230 kW [2] |
| X AWD (MY26) | up to 150 kW [2] |
| 009 FWD | 150 kW [3] |

The 7X uses an 800V system [1], so it can make use of the fastest public chargers. Peak figures are what the car can accept under ideal conditions; a 350 kW charger can't deliver 450 kW, and the actual rate depends on the charger, battery temperature and state of charge.

!!! warning "Sources differ: 7X 10-80% times"
    Zeekr's own 7X page shows 13 minutes (RWD) and 16 minutes (Long Range, AWD) in one table and 11 minutes in another, and EV Central quotes "as little as 11 minutes" [4][5]. Expect roughly 10 to 16 minutes on a fast charger, depending on variant, battery temperature and the charger.

AC charging is much slower and depends on both the charger and your car's onboard charger (11 kW or 22 kW depending on model; see [Home Charging](home-setup.md)).

## Etiquette

- **Stop at 80% when others are waiting.** Charging slows a lot near full, so the last 20% ties up the bay for a long time.
- **Move the car when you're done.** Don't leave it plugged in and finished in a busy bay.
- **Check in on PlugShare.** A quick "charged fine" or "unit 2 faulty" helps the next driver.
