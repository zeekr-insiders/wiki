# Road Trip Charging Guide

So you want to take your Zeekr on a road trip? Good news: the 800V architecture and fast charging make Australian road trips genuinely practical. Here's how to do it right.

## The Golden Rules of EV Road Trips

### Rule 1: Charge to 80%, Not 100%

After 80%, charging slows to a crawl. On a highway charger:

- **10-80%:** 13-16 minutes on a 350kW charger (7X, official figures - expect a little longer in the real world)
- **80-100%:** Another 20-30 minutes for that last 20%

**The strategy:** Multiple short top-ups (10-15 minutes) beat one long charge. Charge to 80%, drive, charge to 80% again.

### Rule 2: The 7X's 800V System Is Made for This - Use It

The Zeekr 7X's 800V architecture means you can actually hit the advertised speeds on 350kW chargers. (The X and 009 are 400V cars, so they peak around 150kW - still fine, just plan slightly longer stops.)

**Seek out these networks:**

- **Chargefox Ultra-Rapid:** 350kW chargers on major highways
- **Evie Networks:** 350kW chargers, often at fast food locations
- **Tesla Superchargers:** The majority of Australian Supercharger sites are now open to non-Tesla EVs with CCS2 - check the Tesla app for which ones

A 400V car (Tesla Model Y, BYD Atto 3) maxes out around 150-200kW. A 7X can pull 300kW+ under ideal conditions (peak rates are 450kW RWD / 420kW LR and AWD). Use that advantage.

### Rule 3: Plan One Charger Ahead

**Always know:**

- Your next charging stop
- The backup charger if that one is broken/occupied
- Your buffer range if both fail

Use PlugShare to check recent check-ins. If the last 3 people said "broken", believe them and plan the backup.

## Planning Your Route

### Essential Apps (Download Before You Leave)

**1. PlugShare**

- The comprehensive map of all chargers
- Real-time check-ins from other users
- Filter by connector type (CCS2 for your Zeekr)
- **Pro tip:** Enable notifications for chargers on your route

**2. A Better Route Planner (ABRP)**

- Purpose-built for EV road trips
- Enter your vehicle (Zeekr 7X/X/009)
- Calculates optimal charging stops
- Accounts for weather, elevation, speed
- Download the route for offline access

**3. Network Apps**

- **Chargefox:** Essential for highway travel
- **Evie Networks:** Great for lunch stops
- **Tesla:** If using Superchargers open to non-Tesla

**4. Your Zeekr's Built-In Nav**

The car knows its own battery level and can route via chargers. Use it as a backup to your phone planning.

### The Planning Process

1. **Route in ABRP:** Enter start/destination, see suggested stops
2. **Cross-check PlugShare:** Verify chargers are reliable (check recent reviews)
3. **Screenshot the plan:** Have it offline in case of poor reception
4. **Share with co-pilot:** Someone else should know the plan
5. **Add buffer:** Assume 10% less range than predicted

## Popular Australian Road Trip Routes

### Melbourne to Sydney (Hume Highway)

**Distance:** ~880km

**Suggested stops:**

1. **Gundagai (Chargefox/Evie):** roughly 390km from Melbourne, well-placed first stop and a good lunch option
2. **Goulburn (Chargefox):** roughly 660-690km from Melbourne (about 195km short of Sydney) - a handy final top-up
3. **Arrive Sydney** with 20-30% remaining

Distances are approximate - confirm with your route planner, as they vary with your exact start point and route.

**Tips:**

- Leave Melbourne with 100% (charge overnight before departure)
- First stop around 80-100km remaining (don't cut it fine)
- Charge while eating lunch - makes the stop productive
- Check for roadworks/traffic before you leave

**Typical charging time:** 2-3 stops × 15-20 minutes each = 40-60 minutes total charging

### Great Ocean Road

**The challenge:** Limited fast charging infrastructure once you leave the Geelong area.

**Strategy:**

- Charge to 100% before leaving Melbourne/Geelong
- Book accommodation with destination charging (7kW/22kW)
- Charge overnight at your hotel
- Plan conservative daily distances (250-300km max)

**Destination chargers:**

- Apollo Bay, Port Campbell, Warrnambool have some AC chargers
- Check PlugShare for accommodation with charging

**Tip:** This trip is better as a slow tour, not a fast blast. Enjoy the scenery, charge overnight, stress less.

### Melbourne to Adelaide

**Distance:** ~730km

**Suggested stops:**

1. **Horsham (Chargefox):** ~300km from Melbourne
2. **Bordertown or Tailem Bend (Chargefox/Evie):** Depending on your charge level
3. **Arrive Adelaide**

**Tips:**

- More remote than Hume Highway - take the charging plan seriously
- Fill up at every opportunity if chargers are spaced far apart
- Western Highway can be windy - affects range

### Alpine Region (High Country)

**Range killer:** Climbing mountains uses a lot of power.

**The good news:** You regenerate heaps coming back down.

**Strategy:**

- Plan for 30-40% less range on mountain ascents
- Check PlugShare for destination chargers at ski resorts/towns (Falls Creek, Mt Hotham, Bright)
- Many alpine towns have 7kW AC chargers - charge overnight
- Keep state-of-charge above 30% in remote areas

## Charging Strategy on the Road

### The 10-80% Rule

**Why not charge to 100%?**

| State of Charge | Charge Speed |
| :--- | :--- |
| 10-50% | **Fast** (250-300kW on 7X) |
| 50-80% | **Medium** (150-200kW) |
| 80-100% | **Slow** (50-80kW) |

**Time to add range:**

- 10-80%: 13-16 minutes official, expect up to ~20 in practice (adds ~340-430km depending on pack)
- 80-100%: Another ~20 minutes (adds ~100km)

**The math:** You waste more time charging that last 20% than you save by having the extra range.

### Timing Charging with Breaks

**Align charging with natural breaks:**

- **Lunch:** 20-30 minute stop = perfect for a top-up
- **Toilet/coffee break:** 10-15 minutes = quick charge
- **Dinner:** 45+ minutes = can go to 100% if you want

**Avoid:** Sitting in the car at a charger doing nothing. Make every stop useful.

### Managing Charger Queues

**Peak times to avoid:**

- Long weekends (Easter, Melbourne Cup, Christmas)
- Friday afternoons heading out of cities
- Sunday afternoons heading back to cities

**If you arrive at a queue:**

1. Check PlugShare for nearby alternatives
2. Ask how long people will be (most stop at 80%)
3. Consider charging at a slower charger for 10 minutes to get buffer
4. Use the time productively (meal, rest, stretch)

## Range Optimisation Tips

### Highway Driving

**Speed vs Range:**

| Speed | Approximate Range Impact |
| :--- | :--- |
| 90 km/h | Best efficiency |
| 110 km/h | ~10-15% less range |
| 120-130 km/h | ~20-25% less range |

**The reality:** Driving at 110 km/h is fine. Driving at 130 km/h constantly will hurt your range noticeably.

**Tip:** Use cruise control on highways - maintains constant speed and maximises efficiency.

### Climate Control Impact

**Air conditioning:**

- Running AC at 22°C: ~5-10% range reduction
- Heating in winter: ~10-15% range reduction (the 7X has a heat pump as standard, which is far more efficient than the resistive heaters in some rivals)

**Strategy:**

- Pre-condition the car while plugged in (heat/cool before leaving)
- Use seat heaters instead of cabin heating (more efficient)
- Moderate temperature settings (20-24°C) are fine

**Don't stress:** Driving in comfort is worth 20km of range. You're on a road trip, not a hypermiling competition.

### Other Factors

**Tyre pressure:** Check before long trips. Underinflated tyres kill range and wear faster.

**Cargo/roof load:** Roof racks, boxes, bikes add drag. Remove if not needed.

**Weight:** A full car (5 people + luggage) uses ~5-10% more energy than solo driving.

**Wind:** Headwinds hurt, tailwinds help. Budget extra range if forecast is windy.

## Emergency Preparedness

### If You're Running Low on Charge

**Don't panic. Do this:**

1. **Slow down:** Drop to 90 km/h, hazards on if needed
2. **Turn off AC/heating:** Sacrifice comfort temporarily
3. **Check PlugShare:** Look for ANY charger (even slow 7kW AC chargers can get you to the next fast charger)
4. **Call ahead:** Many businesses/hotels will let you plug into a power outlet for $10-20

### Towing an EV

!!! warning "Important"
    EVs should be towed with wheels OFF the ground (flatbed or trailer). Towing with wheels on the ground can damage the motor/drivetrain.

Make sure the tow truck operator knows it's an EV.

## Winter vs Summer Range

### Winter (Cold Weather)

**Expect:** 10-20% less range than WLTP rating

**Reasons:**

- Battery is less efficient when cold
- Heating uses battery power
- Denser air increases drag

**Mitigation:**

- Pre-condition while plugged in
- Park in garage if possible (warmer starting temperature)
- Use seat heaters instead of cabin heat

### Summer (Hot Weather)

**Expect:** 5-10% less range than WLTP rating

**Reasons:**

- AC usage
- Battery thermal management (cooling)

**Mitigation:**

- Pre-cool while plugged in
- Park in shade when possible
- Moderate AC settings

**Real world:** Most 7X owners report 500-550km highway range in good conditions, 450-500km in poor conditions (winter, headwinds, high speed).
