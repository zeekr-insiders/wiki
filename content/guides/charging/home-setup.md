# Home Charging Setup Guide

The best part about owning an EV is waking up to a full "tank" every morning. Here's how to set up home charging for your Zeekr the right way.

## The Reality Check

**Most Zeekr owners charge at home 90% of the time.** Public charging is fine for road trips, but home charging is cheaper, more convenient, and happens while you sleep.

The 7X has 22kW AC charging capability, which means you can take full advantage of a three-phase wall box if you have it. The X and 009 also support high AC charging rates.

## Your Charging Options

### Option 1: Standard Power Outlet (Emergency Only)

**Speed:** ~2.3kW (10A outlet)

**Adds per hour:** ~10-15km of range

**Good for:** Absolutely nothing except emergencies. This will take 30+ hours to charge from empty.

**Cost:** Free (you already have it)

!!! warning "Don't Rely on This"
    While the Zeekr includes a portable charging cable that plugs into a standard outlet, this is genuinely only for emergencies. You cannot realistically run your daily charging this way.

### Option 2: Dedicated 7kW Wall Box (Single-Phase)

**Speed:** 7kW

**Adds per hour:** ~35km of range

**Full charge:** 10-12 hours (empty to full)

**Good for:** Most people. Plug in overnight, wake up to 100%.

**Cost:** $800-$1,500 (equipment + installation)

**Best for:**

- Homes with single-phase power (most Australian homes)
- Daily driving under 200km
- Overnight charging

### Option 3: 22kW Wall Box (Three-Phase) - The 7X Sweet Spot

**Speed:** 22kW

**Adds per hour:** ~110km of range

**Full charge:** 3-4 hours (empty to full on 7X)

**Good for:** Anyone with three-phase power who wants fast home charging.

**Cost:** $1,500-$3,000 (equipment + installation)

**Best for:**

- Homes with three-phase power (check your switchboard)
- People who want to top up quickly between trips
- Solar owners wanting to charge during peak sun hours
- Zeekr 7X owners wanting maximum speed

!!! tip "Check Your Phase"
    Not sure if you have three-phase power? Look at your switchboard. Single-phase has one main switch. Three-phase has three. Or just ask your electrician when you get a quote.

### Installation Costs

**Typical range:** $500-$1,500 depending on:

- Distance from your switchboard to where you want the charger
- Whether your switchboard needs upgrading
- If trenching is required (running cable underground)
- Your electrician's rates

**Best case:** $500 - Switchboard is in the garage, short cable run, easy install.

**Worst case:** $2,000+ - Switchboard is on the opposite side of the house, needs upgrading, trenching required.

## The Installation Process

### Step 1: Get Quotes from Certified Installers

You need a licensed electrician experienced with EV chargers. They'll:

1. Assess your switchboard capacity
2. Check if you need an upgrade
3. Recommend charger placement
4. Provide a written quote

### Step 2: Choose Your Charger Location

**Ideal placement:**

- Close to where you park
- Weather-protected (garage, carport, covered area)
- Short cable run from switchboard
- Accessible for future service

**Cable length:** Most people get a 5-7 meter charging cable. This gives flexibility in parking position.

### Step 3: Installation Day

A proper install takes 2-4 hours and includes:

- Dedicated circuit from switchboard
- Residual Current Device (RCD) for safety
- Wall-mounted charger unit
- Testing and commissioning

Your installer should provide:

- Electrical compliance certificate
- Warranty documentation
- User manual and app setup help

### Charging from Solar

With a smart charger like Zappi or Fronius Wattpilot, you can:

- Automatically charge only when solar is generating
- Use excess solar instead of exporting it for 5c/kWh
- Set charging to start when solar production exceeds home usage

**The math:** If you're paying 30c/kWh for grid power but your solar export is 5c/kWh, charging from solar saves you 25c per kWh. On a 75kWh charge, that's $18.75 saved.

## Apartments and Strata

Charging in an apartment is harder but not impossible:

### Your Rights

In most states, strata cannot unreasonably refuse an EV charger installation if:

- You pay for it
- It's installed by a licensed electrician
- It doesn't affect common property unreasonably

### Practical Approach

1. **Check your parking:** Do you have a dedicated car space with nearby power?
2. **Talk to strata:** Submit a formal request with quotes and plans
3. **Offer to pay:** Emphasise you're covering all costs
4. **Get it in writing:** Ensure approval is recorded in minutes

Some modern apartments have "EV-ready" car spaces with conduit already run. Ask your building manager.

### Shared Charging

Some strata committees install shared chargers with individual billing. Ask if your building is considering this.

## Electricity Costs and Tariffs

### Time-of-Use (TOU) Tariffs

Switch to a TOU tariff and charge overnight for massive savings:

| Time | Rate (typical) | Cost per 100km |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Off-Peak (11pm-7am)** | 12-18c/kWh | $2-3 |
| **Shoulder** | 20-25c/kWh | $3-4 |
| **Peak** | 30-40c/kWh | $5-6 |

**Example:** Charging a 7X (75kWh) from 20% to 100% overnight = 60kWh × 15c = $9.

Compare that to petrol: $2.50 per litre × 25 litres = $62.50 for equivalent range.

### Best Electricity Plans for EV Owners

Check these retailers for EV-specific plans:

- **AGL**: EV Night Saver plan
- **Origin**: EV Plan with cheaper overnight rates
- **Energy Australia**: EV smart charging options

Use comparison sites but filter for TOU plans with low overnight rates.

## Smart Charging Features Worth Having

Modern wall boxes can do clever things:

**Load Balancing:** Automatically reduces charging if your home is using lots of power (prevents tripping breakers).

**Solar Diversion:** Charges only when solar is generating surplus power.

**Scheduled Charging:** Set it to charge only during off-peak hours.

**App Control:** Start/stop charging, monitor usage, set limits remotely.

**OCPP Protocol:** Future-proofs your charger for smart grid integration.

## Questions to Ask Your Installer

Before you book, confirm:

1. Are you a licensed electrician with EV charging experience?
2. Will I need a switchboard upgrade? (Add $500-$1,500 if yes)
3. What's included in your quote? (Materials, labour, testing, certificates?)
4. Do you provide a warranty? (Equipment and installation)
5. Will I get an electrical compliance certificate?
6. Can you help with rebate applications?
7. How long until you can do the install?

## Safety Matters

All EV chargers in Australia must:

- Be installed by a licensed electrician
- Include RCD protection (safety cutoff)
- Meet Australian electrical standards (AS/NZS 3000)
- Have an electrical compliance certificate

**Never:**

- DIY install a charger
- Use a non-certified charger from overseas
- Bypass safety switches
- Use damaged cables or equipment

## The Bottom Line

**Budget for home charging:**

- **7kW charger (most people):** $1,200-$2,500 installed
- **22kW charger (if you have three-phase):** $2,000-$3,500 installed
- **Victorian solar homes:** Minus $1,400 rebate

**Ongoing costs:** $10-20 per week for typical driving (vs $80-100 for petrol).

**Payback period:** Usually 6-12 months compared to public charging costs.

**Convenience value:** Priceless. Seriously, never visiting a service station is life-changing.
