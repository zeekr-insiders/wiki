# Insurance for Your Zeekr

Insuring an electric vehicle requires some specific considerations beyond traditional car insurance. This guide covers what you need to know when insuring your Zeekr in Australia.

## EV Insurance Considerations

### What's Different About EV Insurance?

Electric vehicles have unique characteristics that affect insurance:

- **High replacement value** - Battery packs are expensive if damaged
- **Specialised repairs** - Fewer repairers qualified to work on EVs
- **Technology** - Advanced electronics and driver assistance systems
- **Battery coverage** - Specific coverage for battery damage or degradation
- **Charging equipment** - Coverage for home charger and cables

### Key Coverage Points

Ensure your policy explicitly covers:

- **Battery pack damage** - Fire, accident, water damage
- **Charging cable theft** - Portable and installed chargers
- **EV-specific parts** - Motors, inverters, control systems
- **Software and electronics** - Infotainment and driver assistance systems
- **Hire car with EV option** - Some insurers offer EV replacement vehicles
- **Cyber coverage** - For connected vehicle systems (increasingly included)

## Agreed Value vs Market Value

### Agreed Value (Recommended)

**What it means:**

- You and the insurer agree on the vehicle's value upfront
- This amount is paid out if the car is written off
- Value is documented in your policy schedule

**Advantages:**

- No dispute about value in the event of total loss
- Particularly important for new or rare vehicles
- Protects against rapid depreciation or market fluctuations
- Essential for limited-edition or modified vehicles

**For Zeekr owners:**

- Highly recommended due to limited market comparables in Australia
- New brand means market value can be difficult to establish
- Protects your investment if resale market is still developing

### Market Value

**What it means:**

- Insurer determines value at the time of claim
- Based on comparable vehicles in the market
- Can fluctuate based on supply and demand

**Considerations:**

- May result in lower premiums initially
- Risk of lower payout than expected
- Challenging for rare vehicles like Zeekr with limited sales data

## What to Tell Your Insurer

### Essential Information

Be upfront and accurate about:

- **Vehicle details** - Make, model, variant, year
- **Purchase price** - Actual amount paid (drive-away)
- **Modifications** - Aftermarket parts, wraps, tinting
- **Annual kilometres** - Realistic estimate of your driving
- **Primary use** - Commuting, business, personal
- **Overnight parking** - Garage, carport, street
- **Additional drivers** - Who else will drive the vehicle

### Modifications to Declare

Even minor modifications should be disclosed:

- Paint protection film (PPF)
- Ceramic coating
- Window tinting (especially if darker than legal limits)
- Aftermarket wheels or tyres
- Roof racks or accessories
- Performance modifications (rare for EVs, but declare if applicable)
- Wraps or paint changes

**Important:** Failure to declare modifications can void your policy.

## Insurers with EV Experience in Australia

While most major insurers now cover EVs, some have more experience than others. Consider insurers who:

- Offer EV-specific policies or endorsements
- Have established networks of EV-qualified repairers
- Provide EV replacement vehicles
- Understand EV valuation and repair costs
- Offer coverage for home charging equipment

### Questions to Ask Insurers

- Do you have specific EV coverage or is it standard car insurance?
- Is battery damage covered, and under what circumstances?
- Are charging cables and home chargers covered for theft/damage?
- Do you have repairers qualified to work on EVs in my area?
- What is the replacement vehicle option if mine is being repaired?
- How do you determine market value for rare or new-to-market EVs?
- Can I get agreed value coverage? What documentation do you need?
- Are there discounts for EV owners (some insurers offer this)?
- Is there coverage for battery degradation beyond normal wear?

## Tips for Lower Premiums

### Security Measures

- **Secure parking** - Garage or locked carport reduces risk
- **Security devices** - Dashcam, GPS tracker (though most EVs have built-in tracking)
- **Immobiliser** - Modern EVs have this built-in, ensure insurer knows

### Driver Profile

- **Experienced drivers** - Longer licence history, better rates
- **Clean record** - No claims or traffic offences in recent years
- **Limited drivers** - Restricting to named drivers can reduce premium
- **Annual kilometres** - Don't overestimate your driving

### Policy Structure

- **Higher excess** - Increases excess, lowers premium (but increases out-of-pocket if you claim)
- **Multi-policy discount** - Bundle car with home insurance
- **Pay annually** - Often cheaper than monthly instalments
- **Security alarm** - If you add aftermarket security

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### Underinsuring

- Don't choose market value just to save on premiums if agreed value is appropriate
- Ensure sum insured is sufficient to replace your vehicle
- Include all modifications in your sum insured

### Not Reading the Policy

- Understand excess amounts (standard, age-based, inexperienced driver)
- Check coverage limits for specific items (e.g., charging cables may have a limit)
- Know what's excluded (e.g., battery wear and tear is typically not covered)

### Delayed Reporting

- Report accidents or damage immediately
- Delays can complicate claims or lead to denial
- Even minor incidents should be reported

### Assuming Coverage

- Don't assume your old policy terms apply to a new EV
- Check if modifications void any warranties
- Confirm coverage before you take delivery of the car

## After an Accident

### Immediate Steps

1. Ensure safety - Move to safe location if possible
2. Call emergency services if anyone is injured
3. Document everything - Photos of damage, other vehicles, scene
4. Exchange details with other parties
5. Report to police if required (injury, hit-and-run, suspected DUI)
6. Contact your insurer as soon as possible

### EV-Specific Considerations

- **High voltage hazard** - First responders should be aware it's an EV
- **Towing** - EVs require flatbed towing, not conventional tow trucks
- **Storage** - Damaged EVs should be stored away from buildings due to battery fire risk
- **Repairs** - Insist on EV-qualified repairer or manufacturer-approved facility
