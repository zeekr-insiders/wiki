# Paint & Window Protection

Protecting your Zeekr's finish from Australian conditions is one of the most common first investments for new owners. This guide covers the main protection options: Paint Protection Film (PPF), ceramic coating, and window tinting.

## Paint Protection Film (PPF)

### What Is PPF?

Paint Protection Film (also called clear bra) is a thick, transparent urethane film applied to painted surfaces. It provides physical protection against:

- Stone chips and road debris
- Light scratches and scuffs
- Bug impacts and bird droppings
- Minor abrasions from washing
- UV damage and oxidation (premium films)

**Key property:** Quality PPF has self-healing properties - minor scratches disappear with heat (sun or hot water).

### Types of Coverage

**Full Vehicle PPF**

- Covers entire painted surface including roof
- Maximum protection and resale value preservation
- Cost: $6,000-$10,000+ depending on vehicle size and film quality
- Installation time: 3-5 days
- Best for: Owners keeping vehicle long-term, dark colours that show chips easily

**Partial Front Coverage (Most Popular)**

Typical front clip package includes:

- Full bonnet
- Full front bumper
- Front guards (partial or full)
- Side mirrors
- Headlights (optional)
- Door edges and handles (optional)

Cost: $2,500-$4,000

**High-Impact Areas Only (Budget Option)**

- Bonnet leading edge
- Front bumper edge and lower section
- Side mirror faces
- Door handle cups
- Behind door handles
- Door edges

Cost: $1,200-$2,000

### Is PPF Worth It?

**Consider PPF if:**

- You plan to keep the car for 5+ years
- You frequently drive on highways or rural roads
- Your car is a dark colour (shows chips more readily)
- You want to maintain pristine appearance
- Resale value is important to you

**PPF may not be necessary if:**

- You trade vehicles frequently (2-3 years)
- You primarily drive in urban areas at low speeds
- Budget is very tight - other protection options offer better value
- You don't mind minor chips and marks

**Australian context:**

- Our roads often have loose gravel and debris
- Long highway distances mean more stone chip exposure
- Harsh sun can degrade paint over time (UV-resistant PPF helps)
- Bugs and bird droppings are aggressive - PPF makes cleaning easier

### Popular PPF Brands

- **XPEL Ultimate Plus** - Industry leading, excellent self-healing
- **3M Scotchgard Pro** - Strong reputation, good warranty
- **STEK DYNOshield** - Excellent clarity and self-healing
- **Suntek Ultra** - Good mid-range option

**Warranty:** Quality films come with 7-10 year warranties against yellowing, cracking, and peeling.

### Installation Considerations

- **Paint condition** - PPF should only be applied to perfect paint. May require paint correction first ($500-$1,500).
- **Installation time** - Full vehicle: 3-5 days. Partial: 1-2 days.
- **Curing time** - May show slight haze or bubbles for 1-2 weeks as film cures
- **Edges** - Wrapped edges (film tucked under panels) last longer than cut edges
- **Seams** - Quality installation minimises visible seams

## Ceramic Coating

### What Is Ceramic Coating?

Ceramic coating is a liquid polymer that chemically bonds with your car's paint, creating a permanent protective layer. It provides:

- Hydrophobic effect (water beads and sheets off)
- Chemical resistance (bird droppings, tree sap, industrial fallout)
- UV protection
- Enhanced gloss and depth
- Easier cleaning (dirt doesn't bond as easily)

**What it doesn't do:**

- Prevent stone chips or scratches (it's not a physical barrier)
- Eliminate the need for washing
- Remove existing scratches or swirls

### Types of Ceramic Coating

**Professional Grade (Installed by detailer)**

- Cost: $1,500-$2,500
- Durability: 3-7 years
- Preparation: Paint correction usually required
- Warranty: Typically included with maintenance requirements
- Brands: Gtechniq Crystal Serum, Ceramic Pro, Modesta, XPEL Fusion Plus

**Consumer Grade (DIY)**

- Cost: $100-$300 for product
- Durability: 1-3 years
- Preparation: You must correct paint yourself
- Brands: CarPro CQuartz, Gtechniq Crystal Serum Light, Turtle Wax Hybrid Solutions

### Is Ceramic Coating Worth It?

**Advantages:**

- Makes washing easier and faster
- Car stays cleaner longer between washes
- Enhances appearance with deep gloss
- Protects against chemical etching
- Reduces water spotting

**Limitations:**

- Doesn't prevent physical damage
- Requires proper maintenance (pH-neutral wash products)
- Professional application is expensive
- Won't fix existing paint defects (they'll be sealed in)

**Best value:** Apply ceramic coating after PPF. The coating protects the film and makes it even easier to clean.

### Ceramic Coating + PPF Combination

Many owners choose both:

1. Apply PPF to high-impact areas (front clip)
2. Apply ceramic coating over the PPF and remaining paint
3. Result: Physical protection where needed, easy cleaning everywhere

This combination offers excellent long-term protection and is popular with owners planning to keep their Zeekr for many years.

## Window Tinting

### Why Tint Your Zeekr?

**Benefits:**

- **Heat rejection** - Critical for Australian climate, reduces cabin temperature
- **UV protection** - Protects interior from fading, reduces skin exposure
- **Glare reduction** - Improves visibility and comfort
- **Privacy** - Limits visibility into cabin
- **Security** - Holds glass together if broken
- **Aesthetics** - Sleek appearance

### Legal Tint Limits by State

**Front Windscreen:**

- All states: No tinting allowed on the driving area except the top visor strip (roughly the top 10%)
- The visible windscreen area must stay at a high VLT - commonly quoted around 70%, but the exact wording varies by state, so check your road authority

**Front Side Windows (Driver and Passenger):**

- **All states and territories:** 35% VLT minimum

**Rear Side Windows and Back Window:**

- **Most states and territories:** 20% VLT minimum - not unlimited, despite what some tint shops imply
- **NT:** 15% VLT permitted on rear windows (via Registrar approval)
- **Separate condition:** If the back window is tinted, you must have both side mirrors fitted - this is in addition to the VLT floor, not a substitute for it

**Important:** VLT (Visible Light Transmission) percentage indicates how much light passes through. 35% VLT = 35% of light passes through (darker than 50% VLT).

**Note:** Laws change. Always verify current regulations with your state authority before tinting.

### Types of Window Film

**Dyed Film (Budget)**

- Cost: $200-$400
- Blocks light but limited heat rejection
- Can fade over time
- Best for: Privacy on a budget

**Metalized Film (Mid-Range)**

- Cost: $350-$600
- Good heat rejection
- Can interfere with radio/GPS signals
- Reflective appearance

**Carbon Film (Good)**

- Cost: $400-$650
- Excellent heat rejection without metal
- No signal interference
- Matte black appearance, no fading

**Ceramic Film (Best)**

- Cost: $600-$1,000+
- Superior heat rejection (up to 80% infrared blocked)
- No signal interference
- Maximum UV protection
- Maintains clarity and colour neutrality
- Most durable, won't fade or purple
- Brands: 3M Crystalline, Llumar CTX, XPEL Prime XR Plus, Suntek CXP

### Heat Rejection in Australia

**Why it matters:**

- Australian summers are brutal
- Dark-coloured interiors absorb significant heat
- Air conditioning works harder without tint
- Range is affected by A/C usage in EVs

**Recommended tint specs for Australia:**

- Front windows: 35% VLT (legal limit) with high IR rejection ceramic film
- Rear windows: 15-20% VLT ceramic for maximum heat and privacy
- Windscreen: Ceramic visor strip (top 10%) for glare reduction

**Expected results:**

- 10-15°C cooler cabin on hot days
- Reduced A/C usage (better range)
- Less sun glare when driving
- Protected interior materials

### Installation Quality Matters

**What to look for:**

- Clean environment (dust-free installation bay)
- Computer-cut film (pre-cut patterns for your exact vehicle)
- Single-piece application on rear window (no seams)
- Clean edges around window seals and defrosters
- Warranty on film and installation (lifetime for quality ceramic)

**Warning signs of poor work:**

- Visible dust, bubbles, or debris under film
- Gaps at window edges
- Creases or folds
- Inconsistent colour between windows
- Purple or fading tint (cheap film or old stock)

### Tinting Maintenance

- Wait 3-7 days before rolling windows down (curing time)
- Clean with soft cloth and ammonia-free cleaner
- Avoid abrasive materials on tinted windows
- Quality ceramic film should last the life of the vehicle

## When to Apply Protection

**Ideal timeline:**

1. **Immediately after delivery** - Paint is perfect, no need for correction
2. **Window tint first** - Prevents film from pulling during tint installation
3. **Paint correction** - If needed (usually not on new cars)
4. **PPF application** - 1-3 days depending on coverage
5. **Ceramic coating** - After PPF has cured (1-2 weeks)

**Can you wait?** Yes, but every day without protection risks chips and marks that will be sealed under PPF or ceramic.

## Common Questions

### Should I PPF a white car?

White shows damage less than dark colours, but:

- Still benefits from protection
- Chips expose primer (white or grey) which stands out
- Maintains resale value
- Consider partial coverage (front clip) rather than full vehicle

### Can PPF be removed?

Yes, quality PPF can be removed without damaging paint if installed correctly. Usually done when replacing film or selling vehicle (to show perfect paint underneath).

### Does ceramic coating replace waxing?

Yes, ceramic coating eliminates the need for wax. In fact, wax shouldn't be applied over ceramic coating - it reduces the hydrophobic effect.

### Can I wash my car normally after ceramic coating?

Yes, but use pH-neutral wash products. Avoid:

- Automatic car washes with harsh chemicals
- Abrasive brushes or sponges
- Acidic or alkaline cleaners

Hand washing or touchless washing is best.

### What about paint protection offered by dealers?

Dealer-applied protection varies widely in quality:

- Often lower-grade products at premium prices
- May be spray-on sealant marketed as "ceramic"
- Sometimes legitimate but overpriced
- Compare product and pricing with independent installers

**Ask:** What specific product/brand? What warranty? Can you see examples?
